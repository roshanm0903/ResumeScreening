Dynamic role templates with explicit evidence anchors
Use organization controls, role templates and requisition adjustments. Warehouse example below. Check shift, site, start
date and pay acceptance separately.

  Scoring parameter                                 Weight           Example evidence

  Order picking and packing                         30%              Preparing, checking and packing orders

  Inventory accuracy                                25%              Cycle counts and stock reconciliation

  Warehouse systems                                 20%              Barcode scanners or warehouse systems

  Safety procedures                                 15%              PPE and safe handling procedures

  Dispatch and receiving                            10%              Goods receipt, dispatch or loading


Proposed anchors. 0 = no supporting evidence; 1 = relevant task stated; 2 = task with context or a result. Save source
evidence and ratings; compute weighted totals deterministically. Clarify missing form inputs or unreadable text, then score.
Exclude non-applicable criteria in the template.

Routing. Reject failed mandatory checks or scores below threshold; schedule passing candidates. Illustrative threshold: 70.
Bands: 0-49 poor, 50-69 fair, 70-85 good, 86-100 excellent. No ranking within bands. Calibrate before activation.

Scheduling. Offer three slots; accept a choice or find alternatives. Hold, recheck and book once. Bound clarification
attempts, persist replies and escalate unresolved cases by 36 hours. No response or capacity limit must not become
rejection.
Scope. Include structured WhatsApp clarification. Assessments and background verification remain future extensions; explore
earlier checks with consent and local approval.

Design for the distribution of demand
400,000 applications / 20,000 hires = 20 applications per hire; this is not a requisition distribution. Assume p90 = 80
applications per requisition. Use searchable queues, filters, bulk actions and 15-row pages. Test 500-applicant
requisitions and 11,000 applications/day: a provisional 5x seasonal peak over the annual daily average, with 2x headroom.
Validate all load assumptions.

Effort scenario. If 20% reach interview, 80,000 interviews x 10-20 minutes = 13,300-26,700 coordination hours/year; a 70% reduction
implies 9,300-18,700 hours released before exceptions. Screening all applications at an assumed 2-4 minutes each is another
13,300-26,700 hours of work equivalent, not a claim of current savings. Track time to fill and offer acceptance as downstream outcomes.

Batch policy. Start at 12 hours to compare applicants before allocating panel slots, with 36 hours left for follow-up. Tune the customer
setting through experiments; preserve each applicant's 48 hour deadline.

Four month build and release plan
Weeks 1-2: PM, talent operations and local counsel agree rules, country gates, metrics and escalation owners. Weeks 3-6:
engineering builds templates, evidence storage, chat clarification and calendar tools. Weeks 7-10: QA and hiring managers run
shadow reviews and failure tests. Weeks 11-16: pilot by role/site, compare results, then expand only where gates pass.


Release checks and prototype proof
Release checks. Re-score a fixed evaluation set, including the disputed best hire; independently assess samples from every band.
Test prompt injection, extraction failure, duplicate applications, competing bookings, time zones, provider outages and role closure
notifications. Require complete audit records, human escalation coverage, country sign-off and no unexplained quality regression.
Retain a kill switch and manual fallback.

Self-guided prototype. Candidate scenarios cover successful booking, missing details, missing resume, mandatory failure and
below-threshold scoring. Live Gemini assessments, source quotes, ratings, decisions, replies and bookings persist in SQLite; recruiters
can inspect the same records. MCP calendar access and WhatsApp delivery are simulated. Enterprise approvals and production
calibration remain to be implemented.
